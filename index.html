What you are looking at

This page is built from two records kept by the platform: every test a student attempted, with the mark and date of each try, and the time each student spent signed in. Nothing here is estimated — each figure counts actual rows in those records.

The charts below read across all grades at once. Pick a grade from the filter above and they step aside for that grade’s own subject, chapter and test tables.

Click any student in the engagement chart to open their record — hours, attempts, average mark and every subject mark. Hovering any point or row shows its exact figures.

Summary

Where this comes from
The platform’s test-attempt export for Grades 9 and 10. The roll it is measured against is the school’s own platform usage workbook, so the students counted here are the students the school holds accounts for.
What it shows
How many of the students on roll in the current selection have attempted at least one test, and how those attempts divide between a first try and later ones.
What it does not show
Whether a test was set as classwork or homework or taken voluntarily, and whether a student who did not attempt was absent or simply never opened the platform.
Who is not in it
Eight demonstration accounts in Grade 10, named “Student 1 – …” and “Student 2 – …”, which are not students. Every other name in the attempt exports matched a student on the roll.

These attempts come from another grade's file and are not counted in this grade's figures.

Evidence of impact

Across every grade. Click a grade on the chart to open it.

Where this comes from
The same attempt export. Only students who attempted the same test more than once feed this chart.
What it shows
Where a second attempt took students — their mark on the first try against the best mark of their later tries.
What it does not show
Cause. A student who chooses to retake a test is usually one who means to do better, so this is the gain on retakes, not proof that the platform alone produced it.
Who is not in it
Students who attempted every test only once.

Engagement against attainment

Where this comes from
Across the bottom: the number of tests the student has attempted, from the platform export. Up the side: the student's average internal mark — the school's own assessment marks as held in iAspire, not EduBrisk marks. The marks are Term 1 of 2026–27. Grade 9 English has no mark yet this year, so Grade 9 averages Mathematics, Science and Social Science.
What it shows
Whether the students putting in practice on the platform are also the students scoring well in school assessments.
What it does not show
Cause, in either direction. The dashed lines are the school’s fixed attainment bands — below 50% Below, 50–69% In line, 70% and over Above expectation — so they stay put when the grade or section changes. There is no fixed line for practice: read how much a student has practised off the bottom axis.
Who is not in it
No one: every student on the roll has a current internal mark. Students with no attempts are kept, in the lane on the left.

Class view

Where this comes from
One bubble per student in the selected section. Position is tests attempted against average internal mark from iAspire; colour is the student's average mark on EduBrisk tests; size is time spent on the platform.
What it shows
Four things about one class at once — who is practising, who is scoring in school, who is scoring on the platform, and who is spending time without it showing in either.
What it does not show
Size is scaled by rank within the class, not in proportion: a bubble twice the size is not twice the hours, only further up the class. Colour is banded in fifteen-point steps. The hours themselves are uneven: half the students have under 82 minutes for the year, one account shows 403 hours, and five accounts hold 68% of all recorded hours. Treat hours as a rough guide.
Who is not in it
Students with no internal mark. Students with no EduBrisk mark yet are shown in the palest colour and counted under the chart.

Section-wise participation

Where this comes from
The same export, grouped by the section each student sits in on the class list.
What it shows
Participation section by section for whatever subject or chapter is selected above.
What it does not show
It does not separate a section that was never set the work from one that was set it and did not do it.
Who is not in it
Students whose section is blank on the class list.

Achiever-level participation

Where this comes from
Achiever levels come from the school's internal assessment marks in iAspire for 2026–27, averaged across the subjects each grade has a mark in. HA is above 70%, MA 40% to 70%, LA below 40%. They are not derived from anything on EduBrisk.
What it shows
Whether participation differs between higher, middle and lower achievers.
What it does not show
Current standing. The level is fixed from the assessment it was taken from and does not move with platform results.
Who is not in it
Students with no internal mark on record carry no level, so they sit outside this table — they are still counted everywhere else.

What it does not show
